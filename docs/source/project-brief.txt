Rehearsal: Project Brief
Oct 18, 2026  ·  @Soroush Baraouf
Summary
Rehearsal is terraform plan  for AI agents doing batch operations in fintech: the agent
decides inside a copy of the company, a human approves the resulting plan once, and a
deterministic engine applies exactly that plan to production, holding any step whose
assumptions stopped being true.
The user: finance and support operations teams at fintechs letting an agent run
remediation work, for example refunding or crediting 180 customers after a billing bug,
replying to their tickets, and posting a summary.
The buyer: the platform or AI engineering lead who has to get agents past pilot, and the
risk owner at regulated firms facing OSFI's July 2026 agentic AI bulletin and Guideline
E-23 (in force May 1, 2027).
The core idea: separate deciding from doing. The model is nondeterministic, so it never
touches production. Production only ever sees a recorded, approved, replayable plan.
Prize targets: Best Overall and Best Use of Eon. The full design also adds a SurrealDB
Agent Memory story, a fine-tuned model, and Gemma on Google Cloud.
Narrative line: "Era rehearses it. Eon recovers it."
This brief has two versions: the full design as it stands, and a trimmed version that a
team of four can finish well by Oct 25. Build the trimmed one; treat the rest as stretch.
The problem
Agents that write to production systems fail in ways no one reviews until it's too late, and
the tools today either interrupt humans on every call or clean up afterwards.
Evidence (use these exact versions on slides):
PocketOS, April 2026. A coding agent on a staging task used a broadly scoped Railway
token to delete the production database and its volume backups in seconds. Railway
restored off-site backups within about 30 minutes of being contacted; the founder
reported about 30 hours of disruption overall. Don't claim permanent loss or 30 hours
of hard downtime.

Replit, July 2025. An agent deleted a live database and fabricated records.
Georgian's Wave 3 benchmark (n=252 technical decision makers at B2B software
companies):
55% had an AI incident in the last 12 months.
75% use human approval gates; only 29% use policy-as-code.
The top agentic threat is excessive agency (32%), then prompt injection (28%).
IDC: 88% of agent pilots never reach broad production. Gartner: 40% of agentic
projects cancelled by end of 2027.
OSFI's July 2026 bulletin asks regulated institutions for unique agent identities, least
privilege, logged and reviewed activity, approval checkpoints on high-risk actions, and
testing under stressed conditions.
Approval fatigue is a safety problem, not an annoyance:
"Habituation at the Gate" (arXiv 2606.22721): across 11,429 reviews, approval rates
rose from 30.1% to 36.8% while inline comments fell 22%.
IANS: across about 409,000 decisions, a third of malicious commands were
approved.
"Approval Is Not Authorization" (Zenodo): after 20 consecutive approvals, agents
acted without asking 88 to 92% of the time; representing authorization as typed
state cut that to zero.
Why today's answers fail:
Per-call approval (Auth0 async authorization, ActionPlane on Microsoft Marketplace
for Salesforce, Zendesk and ServiceNow) asks a human dozens of times per run with
no view of the whole job.
Rollback after the fact (Rubrik Agent Rewind, Eon's own recovery) can't unsend a
message, and restoring a snapshot can wipe legitimate edits made since.
Digital twins and replay (Pome: local twins of GitHub, Stripe, Slack, Gmail, Linear, with
a replayable log and destructive-call toggles) test agents, but don't apply an approved
plan to production or detect that it went stale.
Offline evals (Patronus, $50M Series B; Era itself) show an agent is good in general, not
that this particular run is safe.
What we claim, honestly: not untouched territory. Our differentiators are cross-system
deterministic replay, stale-plan detection including phantoms, ID remapping, saga
compensation with conditional undo, and (in the full design) approvals that stay valid
through a distilled model.

Full design: the core loop
The core loop is the product; everything after it is an extension, and it always gets built
first.
The model only ever runs against the fork tenant. Production receives a deterministic
replay of the approved plan, and a step whose inputs changed or whose query now
matches new records loops back for a targeted re-plan.
1 . Gateway. The agent talks to our MCP or REST gateway instead of the real systems. Era
speaks both MCP and vendor-shaped REST, so any agent framework works
unmodified.
2. Plan in a fork.
Every call hits a fork: a local Era simulator tenant built from the same seed and
frozen dataset as the "production" tenant.
Every read is recorded with a fingerprint of what came back and the query
predicate behind it.
Every write is recorded with its before and after images.
3. Diff.
Recorded calls compile into a cross-system semantic diff grouped by object: ~
Charge ch_812: refund $42.10 , + Zendesk reply on #5531 , ! Slack post to
#finance .

Each operation is labelled reversible (inverse computed from the before image),
compensable, or irreversible (messages, emails, payouts).
4. Review.
Operations form a dependency graph, built from values that flow from one call's
result into a later call's arguments.
Unchecking an operation drops its dependents automatically.
Policy-as-code runs on the diff, not on raw tool calls. "No refunds over $500 without
two approvers" is one line against a diff.
5. Approve once. Clerk handles identity and roles. The approval is bound to a hash of the
exact plan, so a changed plan needs a new approval.
6. Apply, deterministically, with no model.
ID remapping: records created in the fork get different IDs in production; later
steps reference them symbolically.
Stale-plan detection: before each step, re-read its inputs and re-run its query
predicates, so a newly created matching record (a phantom) is caught, not just an
edited one.
Conditional writes where the simulator supports them (Zendesk safe_update ,
Salesforce If-Unmodified-Since ); otherwise re-read after each write and label the
protection "best effort".
Saga retries with backoff under injected errors; compensation of applied reversible
steps in reverse order on failure.
Conditional undo: only revert a field that still holds the value we wrote; otherwise
flag a conflict, so a teammate's legitimate edit survives.
Receipts for every step: reason, plan hash, approver, outcome.
7. Era features used, materially:
write-enabled local simulators ( SIMCORE_ALLOW_CLIENT_WRITES=1 );
tenant isolation with X-Sim-Tenant  and resets;
fixed SIMCORE_SEED  and SIMCORE_TODAY  so every run starts identical;
/_admin/inject  to change production between plan and apply;
/_admin/faults  for 429s and failures;
era evaluate  and a trap-task suite for the scorecard.
8. Scorecard. About 20 tasks on an Era company, half benign and half traps. Report
destructive operations caught at plan time, operations applied without approval
(target 0), stale and phantom catches, false holds, apply success under 10% injected

Full design: SurrealDB precedents
Memory becomes a versioned input to every plan: reviewers' past decisions are reused,
cited and checked for staleness exactly like database records.
What a precedent is. A reviewer's approval, edit or uncheck becomes institutional
knowledge, for example "accounts in collections get a balance credit, not a refund", set by
the Finance Lead on Oct 3 during run 7.
Don't:
store record snapshots or plan inputs in Agent Memory (LLM extraction is the wrong
tool and burns tokens; use an ordinary database);
let the agent write memory (poisoning risk, per the MemGhost research);
let memory influence the apply step (planning only, so apply stays deterministic).
Write path:
Only the approval service holds memory:write .
A precedent is written only when a signed-in reviewer acts in the Rehearsal UI, labelled
with the Clerk user ID, role, run and step.
The planning agent's key has memory:read  only.
Text the agent reads (tickets, Slack) can never become a precedent. A Slack message
announcing a policy change becomes, at most, a pending item a Finance-role user must
confirm.
Read path:
The planner recalls the precedents that apply to each customer.
The retrieval_trace  ID is stored among that step's inputs.
The diff shows "applies P-12 (Maria, Oct 3)", so a wrong memory is caught at review
time, exactly when it would cause harm.

Superseded: a cited precedent now has valid_until  set. The dependency is exact,
because it comes from the trace.
Conflict: a new uncertainty record appeared, for example a precedent conflicting with
the uploaded refund policy (authoritative document against experiential decision).
Phantom precedent: re-running the recall returns a new applicable precedent, so the
step goes back for review.
Audit: "Why did customer 812 get a refund in September?" The receipt points to
precedent P-4 as believed at plan time, via asOf  and validity windows, even though P-9
has since replaced it.
Gotchas:
Store precedents as knowledge  facts on an entity attribute, never as instructions ,
which never supersede each other.
Agent Memory runs only on SurrealDB Cloud; a backend calls it with scoped keys.
Entity matching is exact on type and name, so normalize customer IDs yourself.
The free sandbox is 3M tokens; extraction, embeddings and consolidation all spend it.
Pitch to SurrealDB's judges: "You built entity-aware invalidation for cached answers; we
extended it to approved actions." It hits all three of their written criteria: supersession,
traceable retrieval, and memory shared across agents (planner, re-planner, reviewer
assistant).
Measurable value: reviewer edits per run should fall as precedents accumulate across
simulated monthly runs (target: 14 edits in run 1, 2 by run 3).
Full design: the fine-tuned model
The model's job is to keep approvals valid between approval and execution, without
flooding the reviewer: it is necessary for usability and never for safety.
The problem it solves.
In real operations, approval and execution are hours or days apart, and production
keeps changing: Stripe events, Zendesk ticket comments, record edits.
For every change, the question is "does this invalidate a step I approved?"
Rules fail on free text. "Thanks!" and "I already filed a chargeback with my bank" both
look like "new comment on the ticket". Holding on every comment floods the reviewer
(which the fatigue research shows is itself unsafe); ignoring comments risks a double

The interface. classifyImpact(step, change) -> {label, confidence} , where the
label is unaffected, arguments changed, step dropped, or new step needed.
Four checks, cheapest first:
During the review window, the model screens every change and routes uncertain
ones to the oracle.
At apply time, the check before each write must take milliseconds, so uncertain cases
are simply held.
The fallback is always safe: rules plus "hold when unsure". The model only reduces
false holds.
Why fine-tune instead of prompting a frontier model:
The ground truth is about this agent. The label is "would this agent decide
differently?", learned by experiment. Prompting a frontier model gives a guess about
what should matter.
Volume and data residency. A 2B model runs inside the customer's cloud (Gemma on
Cloud Run with a GPU) instead of sending every production ticket comment to an
outside API.
Prior work re-runs the agent; we distil it. Causal Agent Replay (arXiv 2606.08275) and
AttriGuard (arXiv 2603.10749) attribute by re-running. Distilling that into a 2B model
cheap enough for apply-time checks is our contribution. Pitch it as "cheap enough to
run continuously", not "first ever".

Training pipeline (one owner, parallel track):
1 . A perturbation library of realistic changes: ticket comments, status changes, amount
edits, new related records, irrelevant field changes.
2. The oracle. It's product code either way, so the work isn't wasted if the model fails.
3. About 2,000 to 4,000 labels, 3 runs per change with majority voting to handle agent
randomness.
4. LoRA fine-tune of Gemma 4 E2B with Unsloth (Qwen 2B as fallback), served behind
classifyImpact .
Go/no-go first. Generate 200 labels and measure how often Claude zero-shot agrees with
the oracle. Around 97% or more means the model's case shrinks to cost, latency and
residency. Struggles on thresholds and free text are the headline.
Evaluation:
Hold out by customer and by change type, not randomly; add a new agent prompt
version as an out-of-distribution set.
Compare against rules plus ignore-list, and against Claude zero-shot.
Measure miss rate, false-hold reduction, latency, and cost per 1,000 checks.
Ship rule: miss rate at or below the baseline's and at least 50% fewer false holds;
otherwise ship the baseline. The demo works either way.
Two bonuses from the same pipeline:
Built-in model monitoring: the oracle spot-checks a random 2% of "unaffected" calls,
giving a live miss rate with confidence intervals. Model-risk monitoring in the spirit of
E-23.
A map of what the agent actually uses: if policy says enterprise accounts need sign-
off but flipping the segment field never changes the plan, the agent has a bug. Keep it
to about 15 seconds of the demo.
Honest risks: noisy labels; a 2B model may handle numeric thresholds badly (route near-
threshold cases to the oracle); an ignore-list captures part of the gain, so the model has to
win on free text; and the model must be re-distilled per agent version (frame as a feature:
every agent version gets its own dependency model).

Risks and open questions
Risks:
Simulator fidelity. Era's compatibility pages don't mention conditional writes for
Salesforce or Zendesk, and dispute support in the Stripe simulator is unverified. The
Salesforce simulator runs no triggers, flows or validation rules. Say plainly on stage that
fork and "production" are both simulators.
Scope creep. The full design is too much for 7 days. Hold the line on the trimmed
version.
Crowded category. ActionPlane, Pome and Rubrik Agent Rewind are real. Lead with
the mechanism (stale and phantom detection, conditional undo, distilled impact
model), not with novelty claims.
Explaining it in 2 minutes. The best moments need a sentence of setup. Rehearse the
video script early.
Model may not earn its place. If the go/no-go fails, swap to precedents rather than
forcing the model in.
Startup credibility. Book three short calls with finance or support operations people at
fintechs this week; one real quote in the pitch matters to the investor judges.
Open questions to settle in Discord on day 1:
Can one project win Best Overall plus a sponsor prize, or more than one sponsor prize?
